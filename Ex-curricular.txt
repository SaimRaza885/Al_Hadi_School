The Alavi model categorizes these activities into specialized frameworks, most notably the "Puyesh" (for middle school) and "Pajouhesh" (for high school) systems.


1. "Puyesh" Workshops (Middle School / Lower Secondary)

In the middle school years (notably at Alavi Nik-parvar), the "Puyesh" system provides a vast array of over 80 elective topics held in the afternoons and on Thursdays. These activities are split between theoretical and practical skills:



Technical and Vocational Skills:

Carpentry and Woodworking: Developing patience and precision.

Welding and Metalworking: Practical engineering and manual dexterity.

Auto-mechanics: Understanding the technical world (specifically mentioned as a course taught by experts in the early years).

Lathe Work (Tarashkari): High-precision manufacturing skills.

Electricity: Basic and advanced electrical circuits.



Arts and Creativity:

Calligraphy (Khatt): Viewed as a spiritual and disciplined art form.

Inlay Work (Monabbat): Traditional Persian craftsmanship.

Theater and Drama: Used as a tool for building confidence and exploring ethical dilemmas (often performing plays with religious or social themes).

Chorus/Anthem (Serood): Group performance for school ceremonies.



Theoretical and Language Extensions:

Advanced Arabic and English: Beyond the standard state curriculum.

Advanced Mathematics and Logic.




2. "Pajouhesh" (High School / Upper Secondary)

At the high school level, extra-curriculars shift toward deep research and intellectual specialization through the "Kanoon-e Pajouhesh" (Research Center).



Scientific Branch:

Olympiad Preparation: Intensive training for national and international academic competitions.

Astronomy (Nojoum): Used specifically to connect scientific discovery with the "grandeur of creation" and monotheism.

Robotics and Computer Science: Keeping students updated with modern technology.



Quran and Hadith Branch:

Interpretive Studies: Deep dives into specific Surahs or the Nahj al-Balagha.

Historical Analysis: Courses like "Dauran-e Ma" (Our Era) which analyze the contemporary world through an Islamic historical lens.




3. Co-Curricular Academic Support

These activities are integrated into the school day to support the core curriculum while fostering teamwork:



Mobaheseh (Collaborative Study): Small groups (3–5 students) meet daily to review lessons, preview upcoming topics, and help struggling peers.

Scientific Festivals: Annual events where students present original research projects or experiments.

Laboratory Work: Extensive hands-on time in physics, chemistry, and biology labs, emphasizing the "practical observation" of science.


4. Religious and Spiritual Activities

These are designed to make religious practice "sweet" and voluntary rather than a dry obligation:



Congregational Prayer (Jama’at): Central to the day, often featuring brief "ethical insights" from the principal.

Quranic Recitation: Morning assemblies start with themed Quranic readings.

Hey’at (Religious Gatherings): Student-led religious assemblies, especially during Muharram and Fatimiyyeh, where students manage everything from speaking to audio-visual setup.

Quran Competitions: Annual competitions held in honor of teachers like Professor Roozbeh.


5. Physical and Social Development


Sports Clubs: Competitive and recreational volleyball, gymnastics, football, and swimming (particularly during summer programs).

Camps and Excursions:

Educational Camps: Week-long trips to sites like Isfahan or the Caspian Sea, with detailed 150-page itinerary booklets covering historical, scientific, and ethical lessons.

Jihadi Camps: High school students traveling to underprivileged areas (like Kurdistan or Sistan) to participate in construction or social service projects.



Khod-gardani (Self-Governance): Students take responsibility for maintaining the school library, managing the prayer hall, or even acting as "assistants" in administrative tasks to learn leadership and humility.


6. Summer Programs

The Alavi model explicitly states that "school must not stop in the summer." Summer extra-curriculars include intensive sports training, leisure trips, and hobby-based workshops that allow students to explore interests without the pressure of exams.
