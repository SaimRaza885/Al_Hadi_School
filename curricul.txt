1. The Academic Pillar (Amoozesh)

The goal of the academic curriculum is to produce "pious specialists." It follows the national guidelines but significantly expands upon them through specific philosophies:

Scientific Depth (Inhadar): Teachers are expected to possess knowledge that flows like a "waterfall," reaching far deeper than the standard textbook. This ensures students do not just memorize facts but understand the underlying logic of a subject.

Collaborative Study (Mobaheseh): A central feature of the academic day where students work in small groups. This reinforces learning through peer teaching and prevents the need for outside tutoring.

Integrated Sciences: Science is not taught in a vacuum. For example, in the Alavi model (Source 3), physics and astronomy are often connected to the "grandeur of creation" and monotheism, helping students see the divine in the natural world.

2. The Nurturing Pillar (Tarbiat)

The nurturing curriculum is considered the "heart" of the school. It focuses on the internal growth of the student.

The Hidden Curriculum: This refers to the values transmitted through the behavior of the staff. Since "people follow the religion of their leaders," the teachers act as "Absolute Models" of sincerity (Ikhlas), dignity, and kindness.

Moral Milestones: The curriculum includes specific goals for different age levels, such as "Social Manners," "Purity of Speech," and "Self-Purification."

Spiritual Practice: Religious duties like the noon prayer (Jama’at) and Quranic recitation are integrated as "sweet" and refreshing activities rather than dry administrative requirements.

3. The Practical & Vocational Pillar (Puyesh & Pajouhesh)

The sources emphasize that "school must not stop in the summer" and that students must learn practical life skills.

"Puyesh" Workshops (Middle School): As seen in the Nik-parvar model, students choose from over 80 elective topics. These include:

Technical Skills: Carpentry, welding, auto-mechanics, and electricity.

Arts: Calligraphy, inlay work, theater, and choral performance.

Theoretical Extensions: Advanced Arabic, logic, and mathematics.

"Pajouhesh" Research (High School): Focuses on deep intellectual work through the Research Center (Kanoon-e Pajouhesh). This includes Olympiad training, robotics, and advanced historical/religious analysis (such as the "Our Era" or Dauran-e Ma course).

4. Life Skills and Citizenship

The curriculum aims to prepare students for the complexities of modern society while keeping them grounded in their faith.

Media Literacy: Teaching students how to navigate digital tools and social media ethically and wisely.

Self-Governance (Khod-gardani): Students participate in managing school affairs—such as the library or prayer hall—to foster leadership and humility.

Community Service: High school students often engage in "Jihadi Camps," traveling to underprivileged regions to assist in construction or social service projects.
